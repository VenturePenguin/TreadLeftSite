Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good cost per kilometer (£/km) for running shoes?+

Under £0.35/km is excellent value — that usually means a £140 shoe lasted 400km or more. £0.36–£0.80/km is typical for premium trainers mid-life. Above £0.80/km usually means the shoe is still early in its life (under ~150km) or wore out unusually fast.

Why do some £200 running shoes have a lower cost per km than £120 shoes?+

Cost per km is price divided by lifespan, not sticker price. A £200 shoe that survives 700km costs £0.29/km, while a £120 shoe retired at 300km costs £0.40/km. Durability matters more than upfront price — which is why this leaderboard tracks real-world mileage, not reviews.

How is the TreadLeft Shoe Durability Leaderboard calculated?+

We extract the actual shoe rotations running creators discuss in their videos — brands, models, mileage logged, and their own notes — then aggregate by shoe model. Average cost per km is each model's average UK retail price divided by its average tracked mileage. No sponsored placements, no lab tests: real shoes, real kilometres.

How can I track my own running shoe mileage and cost per km automatically?+

TreadLeft connects to Strava and automatically assigns every run's distance to the shoes you wore, then computes your true cost per km and flags when a pair is approaching end-of-life. Free on iOS and Android.
